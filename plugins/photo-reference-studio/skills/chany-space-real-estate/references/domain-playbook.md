Use this playbook to build a space-and-real-estate industry direction packet. The current physical site, approved drawings, owner data, and applicable listing disclosures outrank generic creative direction.

## Strategic job

First select one mode, because beauty, design proof, and transaction truth are not interchangeable.

| Mode and job | Decision to unlock | Primary proof | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| architecture portfolio | Can this team solve projects like mine? | design intent, context, function, material, support spaces, outcome | view case, inquire |
| commercial place awareness | Is this place relevant and worth visiting? | arrival, atmosphere, service function, people-scale use | visit, reserve, inquire |
| tenant or leasing marketing | Will this space support my operation? | dimensions, layout, access, infrastructure, neighborhood, terms | request details, tour |
| residential sale or lease | Does the real property fit my needs and value judgment? | accurate rooms, flow, exterior, view, condition, listing facts | save, tour, contact |
| development marketing | What exists now, what is proposed, and what is the delivery basis? | status-labeled photography, approved plans or renderings, specification | register, inquire |
| placemaking or reputation | What role does this space play for users and community? | real use, accessibility, context, program, measured outcome | engage, visit, partner |

Define audience by decision role and use case: prospective client, juror or editor, visitor, tenant representative, buyer, renter, investor, operator, broker, or community stakeholder. Do not infer protected or sensitive traits for targeting or representation.

## Audience and journey

1. **Discover:** listing, portfolio, social, publication, signage, referral, or local search.
2. **Orient:** understand property identity, status, context, approach, and primary use.
3. **Evaluate:** inspect layout, proportions, light, flow, materials, function, access, view, condition, location, and approved commercial facts.
4. **Immerse:** use floor plan, sequence, video, or 3D tour to test spatial fit.
5. **Act:** save, request details, book a viewing, visit, submit, or inquire.
6. **Verify:** reconcile media with site, documents, agent, owner, or design team.
7. **Decide and advocate:** transact, appoint, lease, publish, award, revisit, or refer.

Name the main friction: distorted scale, unclear flow, missing support space, uncertain condition, generic design story, location ambiguity, access, privacy, security, or confusion between current and proposed state.

## Proof architecture

- **Identity and status:** verified project or property name, address handling, completion or availability status, transaction mode, and current date.
- **Spatial truth:** exterior envelope, room boundaries, openings, ceiling and floor relationships, permanent elements, actual view, circulation, and adjacency.
- **Functional proof:** what users do, how the plan supports it, and relevant back-of-house or support spaces rather than beauty views alone.
- **Design proof:** approved intent linked to visible form, material, detail, daylight, landscape, system, or constraint. Do not turn design rhetoric into fact without confirmation.
- **Transaction proof:** approved dimensions, amenities, condition, price or terms, zoning or performance claims, and required disclosures supplied by the responsible party.
- **Outcome proof:** occupancy, energy, wellbeing, sales, productivity, footfall, community, award, or sustainability claims supported by named evidence.
- **Representation proof:** current photograph, documentary edit, virtually staged image, conceptual rendering, or future-state visualization must remain distinguishable.

Classify every input as `observable current condition`, `approved plan or listing fact`, `substantiated outcome`, `creative interpretation`, `future-state visualization`, or `unresolved`.

## Visual narrative

Use a spatial sequence that answers where, how, and why:

1. context and approach locate the project or property;
2. exterior or storefront establishes identity, massing, threshold, and curb appeal;
3. entry and transition explain arrival;
4. primary-space wide frames establish proportion and hierarchy;
5. connection frames show adjacency, circulation, and sight lines;
6. mid frames show function, furniture, systems, and human-scale use;
7. close details prove material, craft, fixtures, and design decisions;
8. view, daylight, landscape, and time frames show environmental relationship;
9. support and accessibility frames remove practical uncertainty;
10. floor plan, 3D, walkthrough, or approved diagram reconciles the sequence.

For portfolios, pair each beauty frame with a design or functional reason. For listings, prioritize comprehension and accuracy before atmosphere. For commercial places, combine truthful spatial proof with credible occupancy and service behavior.

## Directing and capture

### Planning

- Mark intended interior and exterior camera positions on a current floor plan or site plan when available.
- Conduct a pre-shoot walk for sun, access, security, reflections, operations, occupancy, staging, view obstructions, and restricted areas.
- Record required and prohibited shots, owner or tenant limits, people releases, artwork or brand permissions, and intended license scope.
- Select time by facade orientation, daylight behavior, operating state, and the actual story; do not composite incompatible conditions.

### Spatial camera and light logic

- Keep the camera level and verticals controlled. Use a credible standing or seated viewpoint appropriate to the space.
- Choose an axial view to explain order or symmetry and a diagonal or threshold view to explain depth and connection.
- Use a wide frame to establish a room, a mid frame to prove function, and a detail to prove material or craft. A detail never substitutes for missing spatial context.
- Preserve window size, view direction, room boundaries, ceiling height, and permanent elements. Balance exposure without turning exterior and interior into incompatible realities.
- Use people only to prove scale, circulation, occupancy, accessibility, or intended behavior. Direct natural action and obtain the necessary release.
- Remove temporary clutter and personal identifiers, but do not digitally remove permanent constraints or materially alter the property.

### Minimum shot system

| Shot family | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| context and approach | neighborhood or site relationship and recognizable arrival |
| exterior | primary facade or storefront, depth, access, and current condition |
| threshold | entry, lobby, reception, vestibule, or transition |
| primary spaces | complete, level, truthful wide views of the decision-driving spaces |
| spatial connections | doors, corridors, stairs, openings, and room relationships |
| function | furniture, equipment, back-of-house, support, or real user activity as relevant |
| material and detail | approved features, junctions, fixtures, craft, and condition |
| environment | view, daylight, landscape, exterior lighting, and time relationship |
| scale and access | human scale plus factual accessibility route or measurement when material |
| immersive proof | floor plan, stable walkthrough, 3D tour, or diagram aligned to current space |
| representation pair | original current image beside any materially altered or future-state visualization |

For real-estate portals, use the active platform's quantity and ordering rules. A historical suggestion such as 22–27 images is a channel heuristic, not a universal requirement.

## Channel deliverables

- **Architecture and design portfolio:** case hero, context, sequence, function, details, diagrams, team role, constraints, and substantiated outcome.
- **Real-estate listing:** ordered gallery, floor plan, 3D or video, factual captions, property data, disclosures, inquiry, and viewing path.
- **Commercial leasing:** use-case proof, dimensions or systems supplied by the owner, access, location, plans, terms, and tour CTA.
- **Place marketing:** arrival, atmosphere, real occupancy, service touchpoints, practical access, event or reservation path, and local-search assets.
- **Paid social/display/OOH:** one spatial promise, one visible proof, one approved fact, and one action.
- **Brochure and long-form page:** story, evidence hierarchy, plan or map, specification, FAQ, contact, and representation labels.
- **Guided short-form video:** stable route, orientation cues, truthful space, presenter authority, disclosure, and inquiry or visit action.

Verify current aspect, duration, listing-order, label, and accessibility requirements for every channel.

## Claims and safety gates

Reject or hold publication when:

- camera, crop, stitching, sky or view replacement, relighting, object removal, or generative edit materially misrepresents scale, flow, condition, outlook, access, surroundings, or permanent features;
- virtual staging or future-state rendering is unlabeled, or the original current image is unavailable where pairing is required;
- room dimensions, price, terms, availability, zoning, finish, amenity, completion, energy, sustainability, wellbeing, footfall, award, or commercial outcome lacks responsible-party approval and support;
- listing, copy, targeting, delivery, or imagery states or implies an unlawful preference or exclusion;
- personal documents, faces, family photos, vehicle plates, access codes, security systems, protected locations, confidential plans, or tenant operations create privacy or security risk;
- the photographer, architect, designer, artist, owner, tenant, sponsor, model, or other rights holder has not granted the needed use;
- aerial or restricted-area capture lacks owner and relevant local approval;
- crew, lighting, tripods, cabling, or staging obstructs exits, public circulation, active work, or safe site operation;
- accessibility is claimed from appearance without verified feature, route, dimension, limitation, and current condition.

Confirm current jurisdiction and platform rules before release. This gate supports review and escalation; it is not legal advice or a substitute for required property disclosures.
