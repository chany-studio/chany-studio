# Thirteen persuasion roles, not thirteen mandatory outputs

This is Chany's own editorial guide. It treats the thirteen roles as a checklist, not a template, and replaces fixed counts, commerce-only assumptions, fixed page heights, and universal copy ratios with evidence-sensitive editorial choices.

| Role ID | Decision purpose | Product or service adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| hero | Why pay attention? | One relevant situation or promise; the offer may appear immediately. |
| pain | Do you understand my difficulty? | A recognizable friction, without invented suffering or excessive fear. |
| problem | What causes the difficulty? | A concise, supported explanation; merge with pain when redundant. |
| story | What changes, and why? | A process or before/after narrative only within supported facts; omit outcome transformations when unavailable. |
| solution | What is the offer? | Show the actual product, service scope, method, or authorized interface. |
| how | How does it work? | A usable demonstration, real process, or clear explanation. |
| proof | Why believe the promise? | Authorized evidence, actual demo, sourced review, or properly scoped measurement. |
| authority | Why trust this provider? | Verified credentials, relevant expertise, or factual method; not fictional endorsements. |
| benefits | What progress do I get? | Translate supported features into a practical benefit and, where appropriate, an emotional payoff. |
| risk | What could stop me? | Answer a material objection using real terms, limitations, or support. |
| compare | How is this different from my alternative? | Compare on a supported common basis; doing nothing may be the alternative. |
| filter | Is this for me? | Relevant fit, eligibility, scope, or exclusions; no arbitrary demographic stereotypes. |
| cta | What should I do next? | One clear purchase, inquiry, booking, trial, or enrollment action. |

## Compression examples (editorial proposals, not platform rules)

- **Single image:** one hook/promise, a truthful proof cue and CTA in a readable hierarchy. Supporting roles may live in the accompanying primary text or be omitted; do not force thirteen text blocks onto one canvas.
- **Carousel:** first-card reason to care → useful demonstration/benefit → evidence or objection → action is one possible argument. Use the agreed card count, one decision role per card and consistent design. One card can merge roles; do not force thirteen cards or duplicate a video's timestamps as card order.

- **6 seconds:** relevant hook → offer/demo → CTA. Most roles merge or are omitted; do not squeeze a full argument into unreadable captions.
- **15 seconds:** 0–3 situation/hook, 3–9 offer + demo/benefit, 9–12 supported proof or process reassurance, 12–15 CTA. Replace proof with known product/process information if no substantiation exists.
- **30 seconds:** 0–4 hook, 4–8 friction/context, 8–18 solution + how/benefit, 18–25 evidence or important objection, 25–30 CTA.
- **60–90 seconds:** more room for story, method, proof and objections, but no obligation to include every role.

The observed reference may justify a different order or pace. Match the audience's decision and target placement, not an arbitrary hook formula. Keep the wordmark, exact copy, and required disclosures legible. Read narration aloud or measure it using available audio tools before accepting the timing; a character count alone is not timing verification.

## Product and service evidence

Products use the supplied SKU, packaging, logo, actual operation, and authorized feature evidence. Services use the real scope, steps, deliverables, authorized UI, staff or location, and verified qualifications where relevant. A service diagram can explain a method but cannot substantiate an outcome. Never use generated client stories, consultations, results, dashboards, or staff as documentary proof.

Offer facts outrank visual references. Reference actors, brand marks, scripts, music, distinctive sequences, offer terms, reviews, and numbers are not transferable. Keep sound function (e.g. a pause before a reveal), not the original audio. Synthetic demonstrations must be clearly illustrative where viewers might otherwise mistake them for actual operation or evidence.

## Acceptance

- Keep a compact coverage map for the thirteen roles using keep/merge/omit; merged roles point to actual modules, cards or beats. Omitted roles may be grouped with one reason. Show this detail only when useful or requested, not as a compulsory thirteen-row student questionnaire. Story is included in the checklist even when omitted.
- Every retained role has a job in the primary argument. A reference with many cuts does not require equally many paid generations.
- Each claim resolves to approved evidence with its limits; unsupported material is excluded from production copy.
- No fabricated testimonials, statistics, experts, guaranteed results, discounts, deadlines, availability, or refunds.
- Duration, narration, captions, disclosures, and CTA are feasible together. A plan is not a rendered or measured final video.
