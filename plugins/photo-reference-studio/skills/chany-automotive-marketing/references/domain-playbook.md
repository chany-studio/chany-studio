# Automotive Marketing Domain Playbook

Use this playbook to make decisions specific to high-consideration vehicle marketing. It is an evidence and direction system, not a substitute for legal, road-safety, engineering, homologation, finance, environmental, location, or production review.

## Strategic job

Choose one primary campaign job:

- brand or category entry: create recognition and a relevant reason to consider
- model or powertrain launch: establish a distinctive promise and prove why the vehicle is different
- consideration: resolve fit, utility, performance, technology, safety, range, or ownership uncertainty
- retail and dealer: connect verified inventory and transparent terms to a visit, enquiry, reservation, or order
- fleet: prove duty-cycle fit, uptime, TCO, safety, driver acceptance, charging or fuel operations, and support
- aftersales and ownership: improve feature adoption, service, accessory, charging, care, renewal, or advocacy

The job is not “make the car look premium.” Define which buyer uncertainty the content must reduce and which action follows.

## Audience and journey

Map `need or identity trigger → category and body-style consideration → household or duty-cycle fit → affordability and TCO → product and safety validation → test drive or dealer → finance/order → delivery and adoption → service/advocacy`.

Consumer decision units may include the driver, purchaser, partner, children, passengers, caregivers, and the person responsible for charging, parking, insurance, or maintenance. Fleet decisions may include operations, drivers, safety, finance, procurement, sustainability, facilities, charging or fuel infrastructure, service, and executive approval.

Structure content around the questions buyers actually resolve:

1. Which vehicle belongs on the list?
2. Does it fit my people, roads, cargo, parking, work, or identity?
3. Can I trust the performance, safety, range, technology, and ownership claims?
4. Can I afford the total transaction and continuing ownership?
5. Where can I inspect, test, configure, finance, and buy the represented vehicle?

For motorcycles, include rider experience, licensing or training context, protective equipment, passenger use, luggage, weather, and stability. For commercial vehicles, include payload, body configuration, route, uptime, charging or refuelling, service, and operator safety.

## Proof architecture

### Vehicle identity proof

Maintain a source-backed identity sheet for:

- manufacturer, model, model year, target market, body, wheelbase when relevant, powertrain, trim, and production status
- exterior color, wheel design and size, lamps, grille, sensors, mirrors, handles, roof, exhaust or charge port, badging, and option packages
- interior color, seat layout, steering position, display configuration, controls, materials, cargo system, and accessories

Do not combine features from separate trims or markets. Mark pre-production, prototype, concept, disguised, and CGI vehicles conspicuously when that status is material.

### Product and utility proof

- Show occupant, cargo, towing, payload, storage, parking, or access claims with the exact configuration and required equipment.
- Use real dimensions and physically plausible people, luggage, child seats, tools, or commercial loads.
- Distinguish standard equipment, optional equipment, accessories, subscriptions, and dealer-installed items.
- State availability, prerequisite hardware or software, and regional limitations for connected features.

### Performance proof

Record the exact vehicle, tires, surface, weather, temperature, load, state of charge or fuel, measurement method, and test authority for acceleration, braking, handling, noise, efficiency, range, or charging claims. Comparisons require equivalent conditions and a named comparison set. A best-case engineering run is not a typical owner result.

### Safety and assisted-driving proof

- State rating body, test year, assessed variant, category, score, and source for crash or safety ratings.
- Use SAE or the relevant regulator's terminology for driving automation.
- State the driver's monitoring and control responsibilities adjacent to an ADAS demonstration.
- Show operational design conditions, activation state, warnings, handover, limitations, and unavailable situations when material.
- Never convert collision mitigation into collision prevention, or assistance into autonomous driving.

### EV and environmental proof

- Range: name EPA, WLTP, Korean certification, or other applicable protocol; state model, wheels, battery, and material conditions.
- Charging: define the percentage interval, charger type and output, battery temperature or conditioning, and whether the value is peak or elapsed time.
- Ownership: distinguish home, destination, and public charging; do not imply universal charger availability or price.
- Emissions: distinguish tailpipe operation, energy source, manufacturing, and lifecycle boundaries. Avoid an unqualified “zero emissions,” “clean,” or “green” claim.
- Battery: state usable or gross capacity correctly, warranty terms, exclusions, and degradation basis where relevant.

### Transaction proof

For price, finance, lease, rebate, trade-in, reservation, tax credit, delivery, or inventory claims, record the exact market, represented vehicle, mandatory charges, deposit, term, APR or rate, total payment, mileage or usage limit, qualification, deadline, stock, and timestamp. Keep material conditions close to the claim and readable in the target placement.

## Visual narrative

Use a deliberate evidence sequence:

1. recognizable human, business, or cultural need
2. unmistakable model and brand memory
3. proportion, stance, material, and signature design
4. one meaningful use or dynamic capability
5. visible mechanism or measured proof
6. interior, HMI, passenger, cargo, or ownership reassurance
7. transparent next step: configure, compare, test, enquire, reserve, or buy

Brand film can use emotion and metaphor, but product scenes must remain identifiable and physically credible. Dynamic footage should communicate control, comfort, terrain fit, efficiency, or utility rather than generic aggression. Retail assets should prioritize exact vehicle, availability, and terms over cinematic ambiguity.

## Directing and capture

### Static coverage

- Establish front, rear, profile, front and rear three-quarter, cabin, seats, HMI, storage, wheels, materials, lamps, ports, and feature details according to the strategic job.
- Shape panels with controlled reflections that describe curvature without deleting shut lines, sensors, badges, or material texture.
- Match paint hue, metallic or pearl response, glass tint, lamp signature, wheel finish, and interior trim to the identity lock.
- Provide neutral listing or configurator views separately from expressive campaign frames.

### Dynamic coverage

- Use permitted locations, approved routes, professional drivers or riders, insured rigging, appropriate crew controls, and a documented safety plan.
- Use a closed course when demonstrating handling, braking, acceleration, off-road ability, or assisted-driving edge cases.
- Keep speed, lane position, following distance, signalling, protective equipment, seatbelts, child restraints, and road-user behaviour legal and responsible.
- Do not frame camera-car, rig, drone, or road closure work as spontaneous public-road behaviour.
- Preserve plausible wheel rotation, suspension, reflections, shadows, tire contact, road spray, and motion blur in generated or composited images.

### Interior, HMI, and ADAS

- Capture setup and detailed HMI interactions while stationary unless a reviewed controlled-driving protocol requires otherwise.
- Preserve real screen hierarchy, alerts, driver-monitoring state, navigation, units, language, and control feedback.
- Do not show eyes off road, hands occupied, phone use, or entertainment behaviour that conflicts with the represented system and local law.
- Mask navigation history, contacts, messages, plates, faces, home locations, and personal identifiers.

### CGI and compositing

- Match approved CAD or photographic sources, trim configuration, lighting physics, tire contact, glazing, sensor visibility, and real-world scale.
- Label concept, pre-production, simulated UI, dramatization, or CGI when omission could alter the claim.
- Never use visual effects to enlarge the cabin, cargo volume, ground clearance, lighting output, screen, or safety envelope.

## Channel deliverables

| Surface | Evidence-led deliverables | Adaptation principle |
|---|---|---|
| Brand and launch | hero film, key visuals, design story, launch cutdowns, press and reveal assets | Brand and model early; preserve an identifiable product through emotion |
| Social and creators | vertical feature loops, owner or expert stories, walkarounds, charging or utility demonstrations | One claim per asset; disclose access, sponsorship, pre-production status, and atypical conditions |
| YouTube and long-form | overview, POV, comparison, feature, range, charging, towing, cargo, or ownership videos | Separate experience from controlled test and state methodology |
| Model page and configurator | accurate gallery, trims, options, dimensions, feature evidence, price and CTA | Synchronize market, model year, build, price, and availability |
| Search, inventory, and dealer | exact-stock creative, offer cards, dealer landing pages, lead and test-drive flows | Match ad to available vehicle and complete terms; timestamp volatile facts |
| Retail and showroom | brochure, display, POS, QR journey, comparison, test-drive aid | Make standard versus optional equipment unmistakable |
| OOH, print, TV, cinema | model-recognition image, concise claim, readable qualification, safe driving depiction | Review distance, duration, local motoring codes, and fine-print legibility |
| Fleet and business | duty-cycle story, payload or body proof, TCO model, uptime, charging or service plan | Document assumptions and operational constraints for every calculation |
| Ownership | onboarding, ADAS education, charging, care, accessories, service, recall or update messages | Prefer safe operation and comprehension over promotional drama |

Verify current channel specifications and legal disclosure behaviour immediately before production or publication.

## Prompt kernel

Every downstream production prompt should contain:

```text
Vehicle truth:
- manufacturer, model, model year, market, body, powertrain, trim, options, paint, wheels, interior, badges, production status
- authoritative source and unresolved details

Strategic job:
- campaign type, audience or decision role, journey stage, friction, desired belief, desired action, KPI

Proof object:
- exact design, utility, performance, safety, ADAS, range, charging, ownership, price, or inventory evidence
- test method, conditions, scope, and qualification

Direction:
- narrative beats, setting purpose, static or dynamic coverage, camera behaviour, lighting and reflection logic, human behaviour, HMI state, brand cues, copy hierarchy, CTA

Safety and production controls:
- road or closed-course status, driver or rider, restraints and protective gear, rigging, permits, privacy, CGI or simulation label

Negative constraints:
- no model, trim, geometry, badge, wheel, lamp, sensor, cabin, feature, UI, capability, rating, range, price, finance, inventory, or environmental invention
- no unsafe, illegal, aggressive, distracted, or falsely autonomous driving implication

Output:
- channel, dimensions, duration, market, language, captions, legal area, variants, and review status
```

## Claims and safety gates

Before applying the gates below, map them into the root packet instead of leaving them in narrative notes:

- `required_disclosures`: exact model/year/trim/market/options, CGI or simulation, test method and conditions, EV range and charging conditions, ADAS limits and driver responsibility, environmental methodology, availability, and all material finance terms such as cash price, down payment, amount financed, rate, term, instalments, balloon or residual, fees, eligibility, expiry, and representative example where required
- `prohibited_or_high_risk`: substituted trims, unsafe or impossible driving, misleading edits, unsupported autonomy, safety, performance, EV range, charging or environmental claims, and omitted or contradictory price, finance, dealer, or availability terms
- `human_review_gate`: `before generation` for public-road capture, rigs, drones, stunts, dangerous action, unreleased vehicles, or safety-system demonstrations; otherwise at least `before publication` for vehicle, test, EV, environmental, price, finance, dealer, or availability claims

Put each proposed claim and its evidence, limitation, owner, recheck date, and status in `claim_ledger`. A `before generation` decision remains subject to the final publication review.

Block publication when any condition below is unresolved:

- The depicted model, trim, options, color, model year, or market cannot be matched to an approved source.
- A concept, pre-production vehicle, CGI view, or simulated UI is likely to be read as a customer-deliverable configuration.
- Performance, efficiency, range, charging, payload, towing, safety, reliability, or ownership claims lack applicable methods and conditions.
- A safety feature is described as preventing all crashes or permitting less attentive, faster, or unsafe driving.
- A Level 0–2 assistance system is represented as self-driving, or driver responsibility is visually contradicted.
- Public-road behaviour is illegal, irresponsible, imitable, or presented with missing protective equipment and restraints.
- “Zero emissions,” “green,” “clean,” recyclable, carbon, or lifecycle language exceeds the measured system boundary.
- Price, mandatory charges, APR, deposit, term, total payment, mileage, eligibility, inventory, or offer expiry is hidden, unavailable, or mismatched.
- A safety award, rating, test, review, or customer statement applies to a different variant, year, territory, or test category.
- Creator access, payment, loan vehicle, hospitality, or other material relationship is undisclosed.
- Location, road, drone, rigging, talent, property, trademark, music, plate, or privacy permissions are incomplete.

Before release, identify every target jurisdiction, platform, vehicle model, offer, and depiction type. Verify current official motoring-advertising, consumer finance, environmental, safety, homologation, road-use, drone, endorsement, and platform rules. Record URLs and verification dates. Route legal conclusions to qualified reviewers.

## Reference handoff

Use `$chany-reference-board` with the exact central route:

```text
domain: automotive
L1: Automotive Photography
L2: zero or one of
  - Car Photography
  - Electric Vehicle Photography
  - Motorcycle Photography
  - Commercial Vehicle Photography
```

Run L1 first. A second search may use one direct L2 by itself. Do not search separate siblings for powertrain, vehicle use, or deliverable variants. Do not append style, lens, camera, angle, lighting, color, mood, emotion, road, terrain, season, location, audience, brand, model, campaign, creator, agency, platform, channel, format, layout, aspect ratio, or quality adjectives. Carry those as ranking notes and production direction only.

The handoff should state what can transfer—vehicle scale in frame, negative space, surface legibility, reflection structure, camera motion, environmental relationship, feature sequencing—and what cannot transfer—reference vehicle geometry, badging, wheels, lamps, interior, copy, plates, people, branded locations, or unsafe behaviour.
