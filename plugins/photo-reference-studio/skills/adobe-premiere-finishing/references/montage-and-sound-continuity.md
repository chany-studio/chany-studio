# Montage and sound continuity

Use for a sequence that needs time compression, a series of related actions, a change in scale, or a visual argument. A montage is an editorial relationship among shots; AE can produce individual shots and transitions, while Premiere should retain the editable sequence and sound structure.

## State the sequence's job

Describe the change the viewer should understand after the sequence. Give each shot a role: establish context, show an action, reveal evidence, or let a result register. Cut on a meaningful action or change in information, rather than making all shots the same duration or adding a transition to every beat.

In their first-person account of *A Complete Unknown*, Scott Morris and Andrew Buckland discuss combining originally separate scenes to communicate a developing relationship, with audio providing continuity. Our application to instructional footage is an editorial adaptation, not a recipe to copy that film's sequence. [Editor interview](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-a-complete-unknown/).

Paul Rogers describes how changing an early reaction hold affected later audience responses, and why substantial sound design was tested during editing rather than deferred until picture lock. Use that as a reason to compare a reaction hold and a sound-led transition in context, not as a mandate for frenetic cutting. [First-person interview](https://blog.frame.io/2022/04/13/art-of-the-cut-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/).

## Continuity worksheet

For each shot, record source in/out, what it actually proves, the focal point at entry/exit, direction of action, needed reading/reaction hold, and sound role. Include adjacent handles. A marker or screen-coordinate sketch is enough; avoid a complex analysis framework for a simple edit.

Use a match cut when the relationship helps comprehension. An intentional hard cut can be clear. A sound bridge can carry continuity without visually morphing unlike objects. If an earlier approved style requires a particular transition treatment, work within it or identify the specific conflict rather than silently changing direction.

For documentary or classroom sources, separate time compression from fabricated causality. Footage shot at different moments must not imply that one particular click produced a later result unless the source supports it. Do not add fictional UI sounds that imply an action not visible in the shot.

## Test with the actual voice

Place the accepted narration and preserve word order, performance and playback speed. Use J/L cuts where sound can lead or trail a visual cut without breaking an on-screen speaker's lip sync. Record a deliberate silent/ambient interval; don't fill every gap automatically.

When music is justified, choose licensed material whose development fits the sequence and establish a few structural beats. Do not cut every shot on the beat. Keep voice, source ambience, music and designed effects independently adjustable. A sound cue should help a real action register or support the intended transition; it should not compete with a newly introduced fact.

Compare the baseline and revision with the same sound level. Watch once without captions for story and focus, once with captions for reading, and once listening for continuity. If a fixed requested duration produces obvious padding, show a shorter cut alongside the requested-length version and explain the tradeoff. Do not silently replace the delivery duration.

For an explainer, alternate discovery, examination and response. A revealing close detail may need a longer hold than its establishing wide shot. Use one main change at a time when dense evidence appears; simultaneous zoom, animated text, moving characters and a loud effect can divide attention. Test withholding the next emphasis until the preceding point is understood. This is our editorial adaptation, to be judged on the actual material.

## Reject and revise

Revise if shots repeat the same information, attention must jump unpredictably, the next shot starts after the relevant narration, an effect masks weak evidence, or a long silent gap has no visible purpose. Conversely, leave a useful hold alone: “more cuts” is not a quality measure.

Use source and technique evidence at its actual level. A creator case study can support design intent, and a community thread can identify a problem worth testing; neither proves an AE rig's behavior without source inspection or a native test.
