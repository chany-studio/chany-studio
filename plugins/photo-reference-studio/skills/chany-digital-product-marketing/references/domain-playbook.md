# Digital Product Marketing Domain Playbook

Use this playbook to make decisions that differ from ordinary product advertising. It is an evidence and direction system, not a substitute for legal, privacy, security, accessibility, or platform review.

## Strategic job

Choose a single mode and solve its actual commercial problem:

| Mode | Strategic job | Primary conversion | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer app | Turn a need or trigger into a credible first-value experience | Store visit, install, activation, trial, paid conversion, retention | The promise is clearer or faster than the real experience |
| Two-sided platform | Build both sides while proving liquidity, trust, rules, and value exchange | Buyer transaction and supplier activation | One side is advertised without enough supply, demand, safeguards, or transparent economics |
| B2B SaaS | Create shared preference and enough evidence for a buying group to agree | Demo, evaluation, POC, commercial validation, contract, adoption | Product enthusiasm fails security, finance, legal, procurement, or implementation review |

Do not default to feature promotion. Define the job as a change in buyer confidence: understand, believe, shortlist, validate, adopt, expand, or renew.

## Audience and journey

### Consumer app

Map `trigger → discovery → store evaluation → install → permission/onboarding → first value → repeat use → pay/renew → referral`. Identify where the real drop-off occurs. A download campaign needs a credible activation path; a subscription campaign also needs price, renewal, cancellation, and expected-value clarity.

### Two-sided platform

Maintain two linked maps:

- demand side: need, discovery, inventory or provider confidence, transaction, fulfilment, dispute or repeat use
- supply side: opportunity, qualification, onboarding, availability, matching, earnings or utilization, payout, retention

Name which side is constrained. Do not advertise generic network effects when the actionable problem is local liquidity, quality, trust, fulfilment, or unit economics.

### B2B SaaS

Map `category memory → longlist → technical shortlist → cross-functional validation → business case/POC → procurement and contract → implementation → adoption → expansion`.

Address roles separately:

- user: workflow fit, effort, learning, accessibility, and daily value
- champion: internal story, adoption plan, stakeholder alignment, and proof portability
- economic buyer: outcome, TCO, payback logic, and opportunity cost
- executive: strategic relevance, risk, change management, and vendor confidence
- IT or security: architecture, access, data flow, resilience, evidence scope, and operations
- privacy or legal: data categories, purpose, retention, deletion, subprocessors, rights, and terms
- finance or procurement: price basis, total cost, commercial constraints, vendor stability, and comparability
- implementation owner: prerequisites, migration, integrations, timeline, ownership, and support

Treat survey figures about buying-group size or journey length as directional research, never as facts about a specific customer's process.

## Proof architecture

Build a claim-to-proof ladder:

1. `observable`: an accurate interface state, workflow, configuration, output, or product behavior
2. `measured`: result with metric definition, baseline, period, sample, test conditions, and owner
3. `independently supported`: approved customer evidence, audit report, certification, test lab, or authoritative record with scope and date
4. `qualified projection`: modelled ROI or scenario with assumptions exposed and editable

For every material claim, record:

- exact claim and likely implied claim
- evidence owner and source URL or file
- product build, model, plan, role, geography, and applicable period
- methodology, sample, denominator, baseline, comparison set, and uncertainty where relevant
- exclusions, prerequisites, availability, and adjacent disclosure
- approval state and expiry or revalidation date

### UI and demo proof

- Label footage as `live product`, `prototype`, `concept`, or `simulation`.
- Record build/version, operating system, device, account role, data type, and capture date.
- Show `context → input → user action → system response → result → limitation` without impossible state changes.
- Default to an isolated test tenant with synthetic, non-identifying data. If a real source is necessary, use only a pre-approved capture that was redacted before entering the production workflow; disclose synthetic or simulated evidence when material.
- Minimize before masking: delete or leave unpopulated every unnecessary record, field, metadata value, notification, and background element. Mask only information that must remain to demonstrate the workflow.
- Never capture, repeat in a prompt or manifest, or upload credentials, secrets, API or access tokens, session values, private account or customer IDs, or confidential records.
- Treat capture approval and external-upload approval as separate gates. Before capture, approve the source, scope, people, data class, and unreleased functionality; before upload, approve the exact redacted derivative, named external media service, purpose, and allowed transformation. Reinspect the derivative immediately before upload.
- Preserve real processing time. If pauses are shortened, disclose `time compressed` rather than presenting a false speed claim.
- Keep cursor, touch, keyboard, focus, state, and navigation continuity auditable.
- Do not fill unreadable UI with invented text. Use an approved demo tenant or purpose-built, approved mock data.
- For AI output, retain the input, system version, sampling conditions, failure cases, human review, and representative test result.

### Business and trust proof

- ROI: expose assumptions, baseline, time horizon, labour rate, implementation cost, adoption rate, and sensitivity range.
- Customer result: obtain approval for name, logo, quote, metric, period, and causal wording.
- Availability or reliability: state service, region, measurement window, exclusions, and source; do not convert a historical figure into a permanent guarantee.
- Security: describe actual controls and evidence scope. SOC 2 is an attestation report; do not casually relabel it a product certification. For ISO/IEC 27001, verify the certified entity, scope, edition, certificate status, and certifying body.
- Integration: distinguish generally available, beta, custom, partner-built, planned, and merely technically possible.

## Visual narrative

Build around one decision rather than a feature montage:

1. recognizable work or life friction
2. branded product appears early
3. one legible interaction or mechanism
4. visible result in the user's context
5. corroborating proof or trusted voice
6. qualification and next action

Consumer apps should make first value feel concrete without hiding permissions, onboarding, payment, or learning. Platforms should show both sides of the exchange and the trust mechanism, not only a frictionless animation. B2B SaaS should make evidence easy for a champion to forward to other roles.

Use human context to explain stakes, not as decorative stock imagery. Let UI, diagrams, customer voices, and real artefacts carry different kinds of proof. Distinguish product truth from illustrative metaphor.

## Directing and capture

- Capture the native product first; add device frames, callouts, and compositing afterward.
- Use close framing and magnification only when the layout and information hierarchy remain faithful.
- Design for muted viewing with readable supers, then make narration and sound add rather than duplicate meaning.
- Keep one interaction and one promise per short asset.
- Use straightforward transitions. A transition must not imply automation, interoperability, or speed the product lacks.
- Hide credentials, tokens, private messages, faces, account IDs, customer data, and confidential analytics.
- Obtain releases and permission for customer personnel, logos, testimonials, workplaces, and recordings.
- Capture accessibility states when relevant: keyboard focus, screen-reader labels, captions, contrast, text scaling, reduced motion, and error recovery.

## Channel deliverables

| Surface | Evidence-led deliverables | Adaptation principle |
|---|---|---|
| App stores | icon, screenshots, preview, description, localized or segment-specific variants, test plan | Follow current store rules; show actual experience and prioritize the first visible assets |
| Website | hero, use-case pages, feature proof, pricing and terms, comparison, FAQ, trust center, demo CTA | Align every acquisition promise with the landing-page state and available plan |
| Paid social and video | 6–15 second hooks, 20–30 second proof stories, static variants, captions, cutdowns | Product and brand early; one problem, one proof, one action |
| LinkedIn and B2B media | role-specific video, document ad, executive POV, customer story, event or webinar assets | Give product buyers and hidden risk reviewers different evidence |
| Search and demand capture | problem, category, comparison, integration, alternative, pricing, and retargeting pages | Match query intent; do not hide qualifying information after the click |
| Sales and evaluation | demo script, champion deck, ROI model, evaluation guide, POC success plan | Make claims portable, sourced, and role-specific |
| Trust and procurement | architecture, data flow, controls, audit scope, DPA or terms route, implementation and support plan | Do not imply a certification or legal conclusion beyond the source |
| Lifecycle | onboarding, activation, feature adoption, release, renewal, expansion, cancellation help | Preserve consent and make billing, renewal, and cancellation clear |

Channel specifications are volatile. Verify current official aspect ratios, lengths, text limits, safe zones, accessibility requirements, and disclosure behavior immediately before production or publication.

## Prompt kernel

Every downstream production prompt should contain:

```text
Mode and product truth:
- [consumer-app | two-sided-platform | b2b-saas]
- approved product/build/plan/role/geography/date

Strategic job:
- audience role, journey stage, friction, desired belief, desired action, KPI

Proof object:
- exact live UI state, workflow, metric, customer artefact, control, or qualified model
- source and applicable conditions

Direction:
- narrative beats, human context, interface framing, motion timing, brand cues, copy hierarchy, CTA

Required qualifications:
- availability, test method, representative-results language, price or subscription terms, privacy/security scope

Negative constraints:
- no invented UI, data, customer, integration, result, certification, price, availability, speed, autonomy, or endorsement
- no state transition or visual metaphor that creates a false product claim

Output:
- channel, dimensions, duration, language, localization, captions, variants, and review status
```

## Claims and safety gates

Before applying the gates below, map them into the root packet instead of leaving them in narrative notes:

- `required_disclosures`: product build or plan, prototype/simulated UI, time compression, feature or integration availability, metric conditions, subscription price/renewal/cancellation, AI limitations, sponsorship, and the exact scope and date of any security control, audit, or certificate
- `prohibited_or_high_risk`: invented UI, data, customers, integrations, state changes or results; exposed sensitive data; unsupported savings, performance, AI, privacy, or security claims; missing billing terms; deceptive or inaccessible flows
- `human_review_gate`: `before generation` when source capture involves confidential data, security artefacts, regulated use, or unreleased functionality; otherwise at least `before publication` for UI, subscription, security, privacy, AI, testimonial, or performance claims

Put each proposed claim and its evidence, limitation, owner, recheck date, and status in `claim_ledger`. A `before generation` decision remains subject to the final publication review.

Block publication when any condition below is unresolved:

- A feature, integration, plan, territory, or language shown is not currently available to the represented audience.
- Prototype, simulated, or future functionality is likely to be understood as live.
- Performance, savings, AI, accuracy, productivity, reliability, security, or market-leadership claims lack adequate prior evidence.
- Editing, animation, or compositing creates an implied claim that the evidence does not support.
- A privacy or security promise exceeds implemented practices or the scope of an audit or certificate.
- Personal, customer, confidential, biometric, health, financial, or children's data appears without an approved basis and safe handling.
- Pricing, fees, renewal, trial conversion, cancellation, eligibility, or material limitations are omitted or separated from the triggering claim.
- Platform inventory, matching, wait time, demand, provider earnings, or savings is not representative of the stated geography and period.
- A review, testimonial, customer logo, expert voice, or creator relationship is fabricated, unapproved, atypical without qualification, or missing a material-connection disclosure.
- AI performance was not evaluated on representative scenarios or is claimed to be universally accurate, fair, safe, private, or autonomous.
- The landing page, store listing, and product experience contradict the acquisition creative.

Before release, identify every target jurisdiction and platform and verify their current official advertising, privacy, subscription, accessibility, app-review, and AI rules. Record URLs and verification dates. Escalate regulated health, finance, employment, housing, education, children, and biometric uses to qualified review.

## Reference handoff

Use `$chany-reference-board` with the exact central route:

```text
domain: digital-product
L1: Digital Product Marketing Design
L2: zero or one of
  - Consumer App Marketing Design
  - Marketplace Marketing Design
  - B2B SaaS Marketing Design
  - Fintech App Marketing Design
```

Run L1 first. A second search may use one direct L2 by itself. Do not search several L2 siblings to represent several audiences or assets. Do not append mode details, business category, style, lens, camera, lighting, color, mood, emotion, location, audience, brand, campaign, creator, agency, platform, channel, format, layout, aspect ratio, or quality adjectives. Carry those as ranking notes and production direction only.

The handoff should state what can transfer—composition, information hierarchy, pacing, interface framing, proof sequence, typography behaviour—and what cannot transfer—foreign UI, logos, copy, customer data, distinctive branded devices, or unsupported product states.
