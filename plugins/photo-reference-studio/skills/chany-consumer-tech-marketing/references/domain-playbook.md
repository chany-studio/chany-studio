# Consumer Technology Marketing Domain Playbook

Use this playbook to make decisions specific to consumer electronics, connected devices, wearables, computing, audio, gaming hardware, and smart-home products. It is an evidence and direction system, not a substitute for engineering, lab, certification, privacy, health, product-safety, retailer, environmental, or legal review.

## Strategic job

Choose one primary campaign job:

- category or brand entry: make the product recognizable and relevant before active comparison
- launch: establish the product's difference and demonstrate the mechanism behind it
- consideration or comparison: reduce uncertainty about fit, performance, compatibility, durability, privacy, or total cost
- retail conversion: connect exact model, package contents, price, stock, delivery, warranty, and returns to purchase
- setup and adoption: help owners reach first value safely and correctly
- ecosystem: show how multiple verified devices or services work together without implying unsupported interoperability
- lifecycle: support feature discovery, accessories, repair, trade-in, upgrade, subscription, retention, or advocacy

The job is not “make it futuristic.” Define which purchase risk the content resolves and what evidence gives the audience permission to act.

## Audience and journey

Map `trigger → discovery → review and exploration → specification and alternative comparison → compatibility and risk validation → retailer and price evaluation → purchase → setup and learning → continued use → review, referral, repair, or upgrade`.

Identify the user, payer, purchaser, gift giver, installer, household members, caregiver, account owner, ecosystem owner, and trusted reviewer where they differ. High-consideration devices often involve several risks at once:

- functional: does it perform the promised job?
- compatibility: does it work with owned devices, networks, software, accessories, and services?
- financial: what is included and what recurring, accessory, repair, or replacement cost remains?
- safety and privacy: can it be charged, worn, installed, connected, and trusted safely?
- physical: does size, fit, weight, port placement, control reach, durability, and material suit the user?
- social and temporal: will it remain supported, repairable, acceptable, and useful long enough?

Do not compress all risks into one “innovation” message. Select one primary friction and distribute the rest to supporting assets.

## Proof architecture

### Product identity proof

Maintain a source-backed identity sheet for:

- manufacturer, product name, model or SKU, hardware revision, target market, and production status
- dimensions, weight, finish, material, color, seams, fasteners, openings, ports, buttons, dials, cameras, microphones, sensors, vents, feet, and labels
- screen size and bezel, display state, indicator lights, companion app, firmware, operating system, and account dependencies
- box, standard package contents, optional accessories, consumables, replacement parts, cables, adapters, and power supply

Do not combine physical details or features from separate regional models, generations, storage tiers, bundles, or prototypes.

### Use and mechanism proof

- Show the product at true human scale and in a plausible grip, fit, placement, installation, or listening position.
- Show setup and interaction as a continuous sequence when ease of use is claimed.
- Distinguish device capability from companion-app, cloud, subscription, phone, network, accessory, or third-party capability.
- Use diagrams, cutaways, exploded views, signal paths, and animations as explanatory graphics; do not present them as literal photography.
- Mark simulated screen images, shortened sequences, and illustrative effects when material.

### Performance proof

Record model, firmware, test method, equipment, environment, sample, baseline, comparison set, units, operating state, and uncertainty for claims about:

- battery life, charging, power, runtime, standby, energy use, or thermal performance
- processor, storage, memory, latency, transfer, network, or AI performance
- display brightness, color, contrast, refresh, response, or durability
- audio response, loudness, isolation, cancellation, microphones, or voice pickup
- camera, sensor, tracking, location, detection, or recognition
- water, dust, drop, impact, temperature, wear, cleaning, or material resistance

Side-by-side comparisons require identical material conditions. An engineering maximum, peak rate, laboratory result, or hand-selected sample must not be presented as a typical result.

### Compatibility and ownership proof

- Name supported devices, versions, standards, connectors, countries, languages, accounts, subscriptions, networks, and third-party dependencies.
- Distinguish backwards compatibility, beta support, planned support, adapters, optional hubs, and paid tiers.
- State warranty, battery or consumable replacement, repair, return, update-support, recycling, and trade-in terms only from approved current sources.
- For smart-home products, show permissions, household access, remote access, data flow, outage behaviour, manual override, and safe failure when relevant.
- For wearables or wellness devices, distinguish measured signals, estimates, wellness insights, and regulated medical functions.

### Certification and safety proof

- Verify the exact model and scope before showing KC, FCC, UL, ENERGY STAR, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, ingress, lab, or other marks.
- A component certification does not automatically certify the finished product.
- A test pass in one condition does not support “safe in all conditions,” “indestructible,” or “waterproof” without the applicable scope.
- Treat overheating, fire, electrical shock, thermal burns, battery ejection, charger misuse, small parts, magnets, hearing, skin-contact, mounting, and tip-over as product-specific hazards.

### Transaction and review proof

Record exact model, seller, market, price, currency, stock, promotion, bundle, shipping, warranty, return, subscription, renewal, and timestamp. Show clearly which pictured objects are included. Reviews and creator claims must reflect actual experience, disclose material relationships, and remain within evidence the advertiser could lawfully make directly.

## Visual narrative

Use an evidence-rich sequence:

1. recognizable user tension or aspiration
2. product and brand appear early in an identifiable view
3. physical design or interaction mechanism
4. product in true human or environmental scale
5. visible result or controlled measurement
6. compatibility, safety, package, or ownership reassurance
7. exact model, offer, and next action

Balance desire and inspectability. A hero image builds memory; orthographic, macro, in-hand, connection, screen, package, and comparison views reduce purchase risk. Do not use atmospheric effects to hide ports, controls, thickness, surface defects, cable requirements, or accessories.

## Directing and capture

### Still-life system

- Create an accurate neutral packshot before expressive campaign work.
- Cover front, back, profile, three-quarter, top or underside where relevant, ports, controls, indicators, sensors, material transitions, package, and included items.
- Use grazing or gradient light to explain metal, glass, plastic, fabric, ceramic, rubber, and coatings without changing finish or color.
- Preserve edge geometry, seam width, button height, connector shape, engraving, logo, screen bezel, and scale through capture and retouching.
- Use dust and fingerprint cleanup conservatively; do not erase texture, manufacturing lines, regulatory labels, or functional openings.

### Human-scale and scenario capture

- Cast hands, bodies, homes, desks, mobility needs, skin tones, and environments relevant to actual fit and use.
- Maintain plausible grip, cable bend, wearable placement, acoustic seal, sightline, reach, ventilation, clearance, installation, and device pairing.
- Show safety equipment, warnings, adult supervision, and installation practice appropriate to the product.
- Avoid implying that a wearable or wellness product diagnoses, treats, prevents, or guarantees a health outcome unless specifically cleared and substantiated.

### Screens, indicators, and connected behaviour

- Capture native screens separately when needed, then composite using correct perspective, brightness, black level, refresh, reflections, and occlusion.
- Keep iconography, language, data, notifications, battery state, time, connection, and product response consistent from shot to shot.
- Label simulated screens when required and never invent a feature, result, third-party service, or privacy state.
- Remove names, messages, health data, home locations, Wi-Fi details, account IDs, faces, and other personal information.

### Motion and sound

- 6–15 seconds: one sensory interaction or function with immediate product recognition
- 20–30 seconds: launch narrative with one reason to believe
- 45–90 seconds: setup, feature, unboxing, daily-use, or comparison evidence
- long-form: review, tutorial, accessibility, repair, ecosystem, or controlled test with method visible
- Use macro motion, assembly, indicators, screen response, and human reaction to explain cause and effect.
- Sound may reinforce material and feedback, but must not imply nonexistent power, silence, isolation, speed, or tactile response.

### CGI, generated imagery, and diagrams

- Match approved photography, CAD, dimensions, materials, port and control positions, labels, display, and accessories.
- Keep hinges, cables, fabric, glass, liquid, particles, heat, acoustics, signals, and physics plausible.
- Label prototype, concept, simulated screen, dramatization, magnified structure, or illustrative signal when omission could change product understanding.
- Do not beautify indicator brightness, display area, thinness, camera output, internal construction, durability, or included accessories beyond the evidence.

## Channel deliverables

| Surface | Evidence-led deliverables | Adaptation principle |
|---|---|---|
| Launch and PR | key visual, hero film, design and mechanism story, press images, fact sheet, demo assets | Keep exact model and production status visible; separate aspiration from specifications |
| Product detail page | packshot, gallery, dimensions, features, specification table, compatibility, included items, FAQ, price and CTA | Make every image and claim match the selected SKU and market |
| Shopping and retailer feeds | compliant main image, additional views, lifestyle image, title, attributes, price, availability | Follow current feed rules; no misleading overlay, bundle, variant, or landing-page mismatch |
| Social and creators | vertical feature loops, unboxing, setup, review, comparison, daily-use, accessibility | One claim per asset; disclose sponsorship and atypical test or access conditions |
| YouTube and long-form | launch, review, mechanism, test, tutorial, ecosystem, maintenance, repair | Show method and limitations; distinguish demonstration from endorsement |
| Retail and packaging | POS, shelf image, comparison card, QR journey, packaging panel, display demo | Make included items, compatibility, certification, recurring cost, and model differences clear |
| CRM and lifecycle | launch email, setup, onboarding, firmware or feature education, accessory, renewal, repair, trade-in | Preserve consent and distinguish free, included, optional, and paid features |
| Support and safety | setup guide, warnings, battery or charging education, troubleshooting, service or recall assets | Comprehension and safe action outrank promotional style |

Verify current channel specifications, image rules, claim placement, disclosures, and accessibility immediately before production or publication.

## Prompt kernel

Every downstream production prompt should contain:

```text
Product truth:
- manufacturer, product, exact model/SKU, hardware or firmware revision, market, dimensions, materials, finish, color, ports, controls, sensors, display, package contents, accessories, production status
- authoritative source and unresolved details

Strategic job:
- campaign type, audience or decision role, journey stage, friction, desired belief, desired action, KPI

Proof object:
- exact use, mechanism, performance, compatibility, safety, certification, ownership, price, or package evidence
- method, conditions, scope, and qualification

Direction:
- narrative beats, still-life coverage, human-scale context, camera and lighting logic, screen state, motion and sound, brand cues, copy hierarchy, CTA

Required qualifications:
- test method, representative result, compatibility, required accessory or service, subscription, package contents, certification scope, safety limits

Negative constraints:
- no model, geometry, material, color, port, control, indicator, screen, feature, output, compatibility, performance, certification, accessory, price, offer, review, or environmental invention
- no unsafe use, impossible physics, undisclosed simulation, or visual implication beyond evidence

Output:
- channel, dimensions, duration, market, language, captions, variants, retailer requirements, and review status
```

## Claims and safety gates

Before applying the gates below, map them into the root packet instead of leaving them in narrative notes:

- `required_disclosures`: exact model/SKU/revision/market, simulated UI or illustrative CGI, test conditions, compatibility, required accessories or services, package contents, certification scope, safety limits, subscription, price, stock, seller, warranty, return, renewal, shipping, and promotion terms
- `prohibited_or_high_risk`: invented hardware, UI or interoperability; unequal comparisons; unsafe use; invalid certification; hidden dependencies or included-item ambiguity; unsupported performance, durability, health, privacy, security, AI, safety, or environmental claims
- `human_review_gate`: `before generation` for hazardous demonstrations, children, health implications, sensitive data, unreleased products, or controlled test and certification material; otherwise at least `before publication` for product, compatibility, performance, certification, safety, price, review, privacy, security, AI, or environmental claims

Put each proposed claim and its evidence, limitation, owner, recheck date, and status in `claim_ledger`. A `before generation` decision remains subject to the final publication review.

Block publication when any condition below is unresolved:

- The depicted model, revision, color, ports, controls, screen, accessories, or package cannot be matched to an approved source.
- Prototype, CGI, generated, simulated, magnified, or illustrative content is likely to be understood as literal production-product evidence.
- Battery, charging, performance, durability, water, dust, thermal, audio, display, camera, network, or AI claims lack applicable methods and conditions.
- A comparison uses unequal configurations, test environments, baselines, prices, or software versions.
- Compatibility, included accessories, subscription, required hub, phone, network, account, or paid service is omitted.
- A certification or mark is invalid, expired, unauthorized, component-only, for another model or market, or presented beyond its scope.
- A known defect, recall, overheating, fire, shock, burn, battery, mounting, hearing, child, or other material hazard has not been assessed and cleared.
- A health, safety, privacy, security, biometric, child-directed, or AI claim lacks the level of evidence and review appropriate to its consequence.
- “Green,” “eco-friendly,” “carbon neutral,” recyclable, recycled, renewable, repairable, or energy claims lack a defined boundary and substantiation.
- Price, stock, seller, bundle, warranty, return, renewal, trial, shipping, promotion, or other material term is missing or mismatched.
- A review, rating, award, expert statement, creator relationship, or typical-result implication is fabricated, unapproved, outdated, or undisclosed.
- Main imagery shows an accessory, quantity, screen, or result that can reasonably be mistaken for what the buyer receives.

Before release, identify the exact target jurisdiction, platform, product category, model, offer, and claim type. Verify current official electrical, battery, RF, product-safety, environmental, privacy, health, accessibility, endorsement, shopping-feed, retailer, and platform rules. Record URLs and verification dates. Stop promotion and escalate when credible reportable product-hazard or recall information is unresolved.

## Reference handoff

Use `$chany-reference-board` with the exact central route:

```text
domain: consumer-tech
L1: Consumer Electronics Photography
L2: zero or one of
  - Smartphone Photography
  - Laptop Photography
  - Audio Product Photography
  - Wearable Technology Photography
  - Smart Home Product Photography
```

Run L1 first. A second search may use one direct L2 by itself. Do not search separate siblings for an ecosystem, bundle, or multi-product asset. Do not append style, lens, camera, angle, lighting, color, mood, emotion, environment, location, audience, brand, model, campaign, creator, agency, platform, channel, format, layout, aspect ratio, or quality adjectives. Carry those as ranking notes and production direction only.

The handoff should state what can transfer—composition, object scale, surface legibility, reflection structure, negative space, information hierarchy, interaction framing, motion cause-and-effect—and what cannot transfer—reference product geometry, ports, controls, screen, logo, copy, accessories, people, branded environments, or unsupported effects.
