# Art direction and continuity

## Select what the audience must notice

Begin with the shot's information: a person reacts, a material unfolds, a number changes scale, or a map explains a relationship. Place the subject and reserve the actual text/data areas before adding detail. Let depth, value contrast and scale establish the primary read. Decorative symbols should not compete with evidence.

For an adult editorial look, test restrained expressions, clear silhouettes, asymmetry, deliberate negative space and a limited material vocabulary. Do not mistake desaturation, tiny text or darkness for maturity. A mascot can retain its identity while the staging, proportions of the composition and graphic treatment become more adult.

Derive the palette from approved art. Assign colors to roles: ground, body, information, accent and warning if relevant. Compare the actual skin/character/map/text combination; an isolated swatch cannot establish readability. Typography belongs in editable design layers when exact wording, licensed fonts or recurring hierarchy matter.

## Specify reference responsibilities

| Reference | Controls | Must not silently control |
|---|---|---|
| Approved character | identity, proportions, costume, markings | new camera path or background |
| Material/style frame | edge language, grain, shadow softness, value range | another creator's logos, copy or characters |
| Pose/blocking | gesture, weight, line of action | facial identity or costume |
| Scene master | layout, object positions, light direction | factual geography absent from the source |
| Official factual art | exact map, chart, label or CI content | generative restyling that changes meaning |

Conflicting references require a priority, not equal weighting by default. Describe the relevant visible traits instead of collecting famous names. Record observed features separately from unverified guesses about the original font, software or technique.

## Material and light

Choose a coherent edge language: cut paper, torn paper, painted contour or vector shape. Match the edge scale and material detail between face, hands, costume and scenery at final display size. Photographic skin beside a simplified mascot can still work, but inconsistent material treatment needs an intentional reason.

For paper assets, define paper thickness impression, fibre scale, folds and shadow softness. Keep internal folds in the art; use separate shadows for moving objects when needed. Avoid baking a strong floor shadow into a hand that will rotate. Distinguish object-attached grain from a deliberate final-frame texture overlay. Heavy microtexture may shimmer after resizing or compression; evaluate at delivery scale.

Separate light direction, color temperature, contrast and background value. Keep the approved face readable across expressions. A new angle must not invent a new sun position. For stylized collage, camera depth and contact shadows can provide depth without changing the material into glossy 3D.

## Connected views and shot variety

Create the principal view first. Derive a second only when it reveals space needed by the shot. Compare landmark order, openings, prop count, orientation, light and scale. Do not mirror a view with asymmetric costume or legible source content. A generated reverse angle is a proposal, not measured 3D reconstruction.

Use a small floor plan or simple blocks when several characters, camera travel or prop transfers must agree. Select flat layers, 2.5D cards or 3D previs according to the movement. A desk insert does not justify reconstructing an entire room. Newtake's public Blender workflow is a useful process reference; its default service/model choices are not requirements here.

Vary shot purpose as well as placement: establishing relationship, face reaction, hands/prop, map-only evidence, over-shoulder if useful. Merely alternating left and right retains the same composition. Avoid drawing a frame or panel around every character unless the brief calls for it.

## Prompt brief

Write: intended use → subject/action → identity reference → composition/camera → material/light → locked content → exclusions → actual output needs. Prioritize the few constraints that decide acceptance. Keep official geography and exact type in downstream compositing when required by the project. Describe an empty insert area by geometry and lighting, without asking the generator to draw a plausible map.
