# Review, revision and evidence

## Review in the order defects affect the shot

1. **Meaning:** the asset supports the intended beat; factual sources and protected marks remain accurate.
2. **Identity and continuity:** face, silhouette, costume, props, scene geography and material agree with accepted sources.
3. **Readability:** the intended first read survives the actual crop, text and final display size.
4. **Performance coverage:** expressions, gaze and poses serve the dialogue; movement does not reveal missing art.
5. **Compositing:** alpha, edge color, pivots, resolution, light and shadows integrate in the intended shot.
6. **Finish:** texture scale, small defects and export properties survive compression and delivery.

Do not average a factual failure away with a high aesthetic score. Mark each criterion accepted, revision needed or unverified with a visible example. Aesthetic judgment may be qualitative; do not invent precision or promise perfect quality.

## Controlled correction

Use a small record: `asset / target / change / preserve / reference / acceptance test`.

Example: change a listening character's brow and closed mouth while preserving identity, head angle, crop, costume and paper material; proof is a registered A/B and the dialogue insert at final size. Do not regenerate the background to fix a brow. If a pose conflicts with required clearance, a new pose may be the correct scope.

When repeated edits drift, return to the last accepted master. Inspect a small representative sample before extending an entire set. Keep useful rejected variants with their rejection reason when they prevent repeating an expensive mistake; never silently replace the selected source.

## Evidence levels

- **Documented:** a published manual or creator describes a capability.
- **File-inspected:** the image was opened and its properties measured.
- **Visually reviewed:** the relevant asset and composition were actually seen.
- **Motion-tested:** the intended transformation was rendered and reviewed at actual speed.
- **Production-accepted:** the shot passes the project's factual, visual and delivery checks.

One level does not imply the next. A still proves neither lip sync nor texture stability. A creator's sponsored example can teach a procedure without independently proving a platform's reliability. Search snippets and video descriptions are leads; label them accordingly.

## Preserve useful records

Save accepted output in the project with versioned names, provenance, reference roles, dimensions, hashes and edit decisions. Do not expose credentials or temporary signed URLs in review deliverables. Exact licensing comes from the source/provider terms, not from the image being downloadable or public.

Learn composition, staging and revision methods from references. Downloaded templates, scripts, music and assets need their own reuse terms. Where redistribution rights are unconfirmed, link the source and write an original procedure rather than bundling the creator's files as your own.

## Minimal behavioral checks when maintaining this skill

Use realistic cases without triggering paid generation merely for testing: a character overlaps evidence; a mouth edit changes the face; an RGBA PNG is fully opaque; a small background is requested for a large close-up; a provider-specific template conflicts with the user's generator; a platform preview is mistaken for tested native automation. Confirm the instructions yield a concrete next action and preserve accepted choices. Record these as instruction walkthroughs, not output-quality tests.
