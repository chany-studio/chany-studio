## Strategic job

Select one primary event-phase job. A single placement should not combine announcement, schedule, sponsor recap, and emergency communication.

| Phase and job | Decision to unlock | Primary proof | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| identity and save-the-date | Is this relevant enough to remember? | clear concept, organizer, event identity, date basis | save, follow |
| launch and early commitment | Is the program credible and worth committing to? | approved headliner, work, speaker, theme, venue, early terms | register, buy |
| program proof | What will I actually see, learn, or do? | lineup, schedule, format, prior real experience, curator or community voice | view program |
| conversion and reminder | Can I attend with confidence now? | date, venue, total price, inventory truth, access, transport, policy | buy, register |
| arrival and participation | How do I enter, move, and participate safely? | map, gate, time, route, access, rules, weather and change notices | navigate, check in |
| live amplification | What is happening and why does it matter? | approved real-time moment, context, participant voice | watch, share, attend remaining program |
| recap and continuity | Was value delivered and what happens next? | real attendance context, outcomes, community and sponsor evidence | review, subscribe, return |

For civic and cultural events, distinguish attendance growth, participation quality, cultural continuity, local benefit, education, fundraising, and visitor dispersal. Do not reduce every event to ticket volume.

## Audience and journey

1. **Discover:** event identity interrupts attention in a relevant context.
2. **Relate:** the audience understands why the program is for their occasion, interest, or role.
3. **Verify:** lineup, work, schedule, venue, price, access, age, language, format, and safety information become credible.
4. **Commit:** ticket, registration, calendar save, travel plan, or RSVP.
5. **Anticipate:** previews, reminders, practical guidance, and community cues build readiness.
6. **Arrive and participate:** entry, wayfinding, access, crowd flow, amenities, and program experience deliver the promise.
7. **Share and remember:** authorized live content and personal stories extend reach.
8. **Return and support:** review, membership, donation, sponsor relationship, next event, or community advocacy.

Name the dominant friction: unfamiliar format, weak program proof, price uncertainty, travel, schedule complexity, accessibility, age or entry rules, cultural unfamiliarity, safety, weather, or fear that promotional imagery is not representative.

## Proof architecture

- **Program truth:** approved event title, organizer, type, participating artist or speaker, work, schedule, date, venue, duration, language, format, and change status.
- **Experience proof:** real venue, stage or installation, attendee viewpoint, program action, atmosphere, participation mode, and practical scale.
- **Authority proof:** organizer, curator, artist, cultural practitioner, community partner, institution, or expert role presented accurately.
- **Access and operations proof:** entrance, route, seating, captioning or interpretation, audio description, restrooms, parking or transit, age policy, prohibited items, weather and cancellation process.
- **Transaction proof:** ticket tier, inventory status, total mandatory price, deadline, refund or transfer policy, registration requirements, and CTA.
- **Cultural legitimacy:** community participation, consent, meaning, attribution, benefit, boundaries on sacred or private content, and avoidance of tourist-facing distortion.
- **Impact proof:** attendance, reach, economic, social, environmental, educational, sponsor, or cultural claims tied to a named method and reporting period.

Classify material as `approved current program fact`, `authorized documentary evidence`, `substantiated impact`, `cultural authority guidance`, `creative proposal`, or `unresolved`.

## Visual narrative

Build experience before spectacle:

1. a single event hero establishes identity and promise;
2. venue, exterior, entrance, stage, room, or installation establishes where it happens;
3. performer, speaker, artist, work, ritual, or program action establishes what happens;
4. attendee-point-of-view frames let people anticipate sight line and participation;
5. wide crowd, mid interaction, and close emotion or detail create scale without manufacturing attendance;
6. community, maker, volunteer, staff, or backstage evidence reveals legitimate human infrastructure;
7. access, map, signage, amenities, weather, or entry frames make participation practical;
8. sponsor, partner, and impact frames prove value without overwhelming the event;
9. recap frames preserve specific memory and lead to the next relationship.

For exhibitions, separate work detail, installation context, interpretation, and visitor interaction. For performance, separate full-stage geography, performer relationships, decisive action, audience perspective, and atmosphere. For local festivals, represent place, producers, residents, practice, food or craft, and contemporary community voice together.

## Directing and capture

### Pre-event planning

- Build an approved program and rights matrix for every artist, work, speaker, sponsor, logo, music cue, venue area, audience zone, and usage channel.
- Align the shot plan with venue command, stage management, curatorial, artist, security, access, crowd, weather, and emergency plans.
- Map allowed positions, movement times, no-flash or no-recording works, restricted rituals, cable and equipment routes, emergency access, and safe fallback positions.
- Establish attendee notice, consent or release method, a separate approach for identifiable participants and minors, and a process for takedown or exclusion requests where applicable.

### Live camera and narrative logic

- Capture a clean establishing view before peak attendance, then the real occupied state without cloning or multiplying people.
- Use attendee eye lines and sight lines to make the experience imaginable. Do not block the experience to create the image.
- Preserve performance and exhibition lighting intent. Avoid filters or effects that detach the scene from what attendees will encounter.
- Capture wide geography, mid relationships, and close decisive moments. Maintain enough context to identify the actual event or work.
- Direct posed portraits or sponsor moments in controlled zones and times; do not stage them in active egress or crowd-pressure areas.
- For cultural practices, follow the practitioners' rules on who may direct, perform, photograph, publish, caption, and benefit.

### Minimum shot system

| Shot family | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| event identity | adaptable hero with a clear focal point and crop-safe area |
| venue and arrival | exterior, entrance, check-in, threshold, or route |

| authority and action | approved performer, artist, speaker, work, ritual, or activity |
| attendee viewpoint | credible sight line and participation perspective |
| real community | wide crowd, mid interaction, close emotion, volunteer or local participant |
| signature moment | one distinctive, authorized event-defining action |
| access and practical | access route, seating, captioning or interpretation, map, amenity, and key rule |
| partner and sponsor | approved activation, attribution, hospitality, or outcome evidence |

| vertical live sequence | identity, action, context, and CTA in a stable short-form arc |

## Channel deliverables

- **Event listing/ticket page:** hero, concise value summary, approved date and place, program, schedule, total price, access, FAQ, policy, organizer trust, and CTA.
- **Launch and lineup:** save-the-date, reveal sequence, artist or work cards, program proof, early terms, and ticket action.
- **Paid social/display/OOH:** one event promise, one approved program proof, one exact practical fact set, and one action.
- **Email/CRM:** announcement, program proof, practical reminder, change notice, post-event thanks, and next relationship as separate messages.
- **Organic and short-form video:** authorized invitation, rehearsal or installation preview, attendee guide, live moments, and transparent recap.
- **On-site:** map, queue, entrance, access, schedule, change, weather, conduct, sponsor, and emergency-approved communication.
- **Press and partner kit:** approved facts, captions, credits, rights status, spokespeople, key images, logos, and contact.
- **Sponsor recap/impact report:** contracted deliverables, authentic exposure, audience context, measured result, community value, and methodology.

Operational and emergency messages are not aesthetic content variants. They require the responsible event authority's approval and consistent information across channels.

## Claims and safety gates

Hold publication or capture when:

- title, organizer, artist, speaker, work, lineup, date, time, venue, format, language, duration, age rule, capacity, availability, ticket tier, total mandatory price, refund, access, sponsor, or cancellation information is unapproved or stale;
- stock, archive, composite, or generated imagery is likely to be mistaken for the current event, program, venue, artist, artwork, or real crowd;
- the organizer lacks permission for an artist likeness, approved artwork, performance, music, recording, trademark, archival image, attendee, interview, or sponsor use;
- identifiable attendees or minors are used beyond the event's valid notice, consent, release, and intended-use basis;
- captions, alt text, transcript, sign-language, audio description, contrast, access-route, seating, or accommodation information required by the project is absent or inaccurate;
- photography, lighting, cabling, rigging, drone, operator movement, or posing obstructs exits, queues, public routes, stage operations, sight lines, accessible routes, or emergency response;
- public communication conflicts with the approved crowd, weather, transport, cancellation, or emergency plan;
- a cultural practice, sacred or private moment, community identity, costume, language, or heritage is commercialized without appropriate authority, consent, attribution, boundaries, and meaningful community benefit;
- urgency, popularity, scarcity, attendance, impact, or sponsor-value claims lack current evidence;
- paid, gifted, employee, affiliate, sponsor, or creator relationships are not disclosed as required.

Confirm current jurisdiction, venue, platform, ticketing, contractual, cultural, access, and operational requirements. The skill supports responsible review but cannot certify law or event safety.
