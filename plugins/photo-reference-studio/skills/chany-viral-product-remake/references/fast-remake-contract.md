## Transfer test

A mechanism is transferable only when it can be described without the reference brand's pixels, copy, talent, music, or distinctive frame sequence. Good descriptions include “macro material texture becomes the product silhouette,” “three escalating close-ups lead to a clean pack reveal,” and “impact sound marks the reveal.” Bad descriptions reproduce a creator's exact frame, camera path, branded prop, lyrics, narration, or shot-by-shot sequence.

The new film must have a reason that fits the user's product. Map every borrowed mechanism to one real product feature, use occasion, customer tension, or sensory property. If the visual metaphor has no product truth behind it, replace it instead of copying it.

## Fast beat limit

Default to three to six beats for a simple product-viral Reel:

1. sensory or problem hook
2. unexpected transformation or product entrance
3. product recognition
4. one benefit or material proof
5. clean hero reveal
6. brand and action line

This is a compression guide, not a mandatory six-scene template. Preserve the observed pacing function, not exact timestamps. Use fewer beats when the reference is a single continuous reveal.

## Product replacement acceptance

For an authorized swap, reject the result when any of these fail: silhouette and proportions, cap/opening/handle geometry, label spelling and placement, material and transparency, lighting/reflection integration, hands/contact/occlusion, source background stability, motion continuity, source duration, audio continuity, or beginning/end frames. A successful provider job is not visual acceptance.

For an original remake, also reject a result that could plausibly be mistaken for the reference creator's branded film. Change at least the setting or material world, reveal device, camera grammar, and ending composition while preserving only the abstract marketing mechanism.
